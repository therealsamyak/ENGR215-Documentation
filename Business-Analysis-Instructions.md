## Business Model & Value Capture Blueprint

### UCLA ENGR 215 – Entrepreneurship for Engineers

By Week 5, your team should now begin answering one of the most important startup questions:

### How does this venture actually become a business?

This assignment is not about refining your problem statement or revisiting prior work. It is specifically focused on **business model design, value capture, and venture logic**.

A startup can solve a real problem and still fail if the founders cannot clearly explain:

### Who pays, why they pay, and why the model could work.

This week is about moving from:

**“Interesting idea”**  
to  
**“Potentially viable business”**

---

## Assignment Objective

Develop a focused business model blueprint that explains:

* What value your startup creates  
* How your startup captures value  
* Why customers would pay  
* What revenue model best fits  
* How the business could scale  
* What major business model risks exist

## Required Sections

### Section 1 – Core Value Proposition

**What value does your startup create?**

Define the practical value your venture delivers.

Examples include:

* Saves money  
* Saves time  
* Improves convenience  
* Reduces risk  
* Enhances performance  
* Creates access  
* Solves a recurring pain point

**Important:** Be specific.

**Weak:** “Our platform helps users.”

**Strong:** “Our platform helps small retailers reduce inventory waste through AI-driven demand forecasting.”

---

### Section 2 – Revenue Model

**How does your startup make money?**

Clearly explain:

* Who pays?  
* What exactly are they paying for?  
* How often do they pay?  
* Why will they continue paying?

Possible models include:

* Subscription  
* SaaS  
* Licensing  
* Marketplace commission  
* Transaction fees  
* Freemium  
* Enterprise contracts  
* Hardware plus recurring software

**Important:** Do not just name a model. Justify why it fits your venture.

---

### Section 3 – Customer Payment Logic

**Why will customers actually pay?**

This section is critical. You must explain:

* What pain is strong enough to justify spending?  
* Is this a “nice to have” or a “must have”?  
* What alternatives exist today?  
* Why is your offering economically compelling?

The goal is to demonstrate that your startup is solving a problem with real payment potential, not just hypothetical interest.

---

### Section 4 – Scale Potential

**How could this business grow?**

Discuss:

* Could this expand geographically?  
* Could it scale digitally?  
* Could it grow through enterprise adoption?  
* Could it become recurring revenue?  
* Is it niche or mass market?

**Important:** Not every startup should be “the next unicorn.” Your task is to show realistic scale logic.

---

### Section 5 – Business Model Risks

**Why might this business model fail even if the idea sounds strong?**

Identify at least **3 major business model risks**, such as:

* Weak willingness to pay  
* High customer churn  
* Competitive imitation  
* Low differentiation  
* Pricing pressure  
* Poor retention  
* Platform dependency  
* Regulatory barriers

The goal is to show strategic maturity. Strong founders think beyond upside.

---

### Section 6 – Business Model Summary

**In one paragraph:**

Explain why this startup could realistically become sustainable.

This is your chance to answer:

### Why does this business deserve to exist?

---

## Recommended Framework

**Value Creation → Revenue Model → Payment Logic → Scale Potential → Risks**

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Weak:** “Everyone could use this.”

**Weak:** “We’ll monetize later.”

**Weak:** “Ads.”

**Strong:** “We use a B2B SaaS subscription targeting compliance-heavy logistics providers with recurring operational pain.”
