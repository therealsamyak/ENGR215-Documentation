This week’s assignment focuses on one of the most important—and often overlooked—parts of building a startup: the people.
Many startup ideas fail not because the technology is impossible, but because the team lacks the right structure, skills, leadership alignment, execution capability, or ability to adapt as the company grows.
In this assignment, your team will analyze the human side of your startup venture and begin thinking strategically about founder roles, operational responsibilities, future hiring needs, and capability gaps.

Assignment Goal
Your team should define:
Who is responsible for what
Why each founder/team member is a good fit for their role
What important skills are currently missing
What additional people may need to be hired in the future
What organizational or leadership challenges may emerge as the startup grows
This is not a technical assignment. This is a startup execution and leadership assignment.

What Your Team Needs to Submit
Submit one team document. Recommended length: approximately 3–5 pages.
Your submission should include the following sections.
1. Startup Team Overview
Briefly describe your startup idea again in 1–2 short paragraphs so the assignment can stand on its own.
Then provide a high-level overview of your current startup team. Include:
Team member names
Academic/professional backgrounds
Relevant skills or experiences
Why this combination of people is appropriate for the startup
You do not need resumes. Focus on relevance to the startup.
2. Founder and Team Roles
Define the role of each current team member.
Examples may include:
CEO
CTO
COO
Product Lead
Marketing Lead
Operations Lead
AI/Engineering Lead
Business Development Lead
Customer Success Lead
For each role, explain:
Responsibilities
Why that person fits the role
How the responsibilities support startup execution
Important: Avoid vague descriptions such as “everyone does everything” or “we all share responsibilities equally.” Real startups require ownership and accountability.
3. Skill Gaps and Weaknesses
No startup team is complete. Identify areas where your team currently lacks expertise, experience, or capacity.
Examples may include:
Legal/IP knowledge
Marketing expertise
Enterprise sales
Manufacturing experience
Regulatory knowledge
UX/UI design
Financial planning
AI engineering depth
Industry connections
For each gap:
Explain why it matters
Explain the risks if the gap is not addressed
4. Future Hiring Plan
Imagine your startup begins gaining traction. What additional talent would you likely need over the next 1–3 years?
Examples may include:
Software engineers
Sales staff
Customer support
Compliance specialists
Product managers
Manufacturing engineers
Designers
Operations managers
For each future role:
Explain why the role becomes important
Explain roughly when the startup would need this role
Explain how this role supports growth
Do not create unrealistic hiring plans with hundreds of employees. Think like an early-stage startup with limited resources.
5. Founder and Team Challenges
Discuss potential human-side challenges your startup may face.
Examples may include:
Founder disagreements
Unequal workload
Leadership conflict
Burnout
Hiring difficulties
Remote collaboration challenges
Lack of business experience
Balancing technical vs. business priorities
Discuss:
Which challenges are most likely
Why they may occur
How the team may attempt to manage or reduce these risks
6. Reflection
Provide a short team reflection of approximately 1–2 paragraphs.
Discuss:
What your team learned from this exercise
What surprised you
Whether this changed how you think about startups
What concerns you most about building a real startup team

Submission Requirements
One submission per team
Submit as PDF, Word, or PowerPoint
Clearly include all team member names
Use professional formatting
Slides are allowed, but clear written explanations are still required

Important Notes
This assignment is intended to help your team think realistically about startup execution, not just startup ideas.
Early-stage startups often fail because of team and leadership problems rather than technology problems.
There is no single correct organizational structure.
Your startup idea may continue evolving after this assignment. That is expected.
Strong entrepreneurial teams continuously reassess talent, leadership, and execution needs.
